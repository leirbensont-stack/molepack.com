A good EDC knife is the most-used tool most people carry. It opens boxes, cuts zip ties, strips wire, peels fruit, and — in a pinch — saves the day. And you don't need to spend $200 on a "grail" knife to get one that performs. The sub-$100 category is where the real value lives.

We bought 5 of the most-recommended EDC knives under $100, carried them daily for 60 days, and put them through hundreds of cutting tasks. Here's what held an edge, what held up, and what's actually worth your money.

Our testing methodology: Each knife was carried daily for 60 days, used for 10+ cutting tasks per week (cardboard, zip ties, paracord, food prep), tested for edge retention with a standardized cardboard-cutting sequence, and checked for lock-up and blade play after a 3-foot drop onto concrete. We sharpened each blade to a consistent edge before starting.

Buying Guide: How to Choose an EDC Knife

Blade Steel: The Heart of the Knife

Steel is the single biggest factor in how a knife performs. D2 holds an edge for a long time but is more prone to rust and harder to sharpen. VG-10 and 14C28N balance edge retention with corrosion resistance and ease of sharpening. AUS-8 is softer and needs more frequent touch-ups, but it's nearly impossible to chip and sharpens in seconds. For a first EDC knife, D2 or VG-10 is the sweet spot.

Lock Type Matters for Safety

  • Liner lock: Simple, reliable, one-hand operation — most common.
  • Frame lock: Same concept but the handle itself locks the blade — very strong.
  • Back lock: Traditional, extremely secure, but often needs two hands to close.

Blade Length and Local Laws

A 2.5–3 inch blade is ideal for EDC — long enough for most tasks, short enough to stay legal in most jurisdictions. Always check local knife laws, especially regarding assisted opening and blade length, before you carry.

Assisted vs. Manual Opening

Assisted knives (like the Kershaw Leek) open with a spring once you start the motion — fast and satisfying, but restricted in some cities. Manual knives (flippers, thumb holes, thumb studs) are just as fast with practice and legal in more places.

Frequently Asked Questions

What blade steel is best for an EDC knife?

For everyday carry, D2, VG-10, and 14C28N are excellent mid-range steels that balance edge retention, ease of sharpening, and corrosion resistance. Premium steels like S30V hold an edge longer but cost more and are harder to sharpen. For most people, D2 or VG-10 is the sweet spot.

Are folding knives legal to carry?

Laws vary by location. In most US states, a manual or assisted folding knife with a blade under 3.5 inches is legal to carry, but many cities restrict blade length or assisted opening. Always check your local laws before carrying.

What is a good blade length for EDC?

For everyday carry, a 2.5 to 3 inch blade is ideal — long enough for most cutting tasks but short enough to be legal and comfortable in most areas.

How often should I sharpen my EDC knife?

With daily light use, touch up the edge every 1-2 weeks with a fine ceramic or strop. A full sharpening is only needed every few months. D2 and VG-10 hold an edge longer; AUS-8 needs more frequent touch-ups but sharpens much faster.
