We bought 5 of the most recommended flashlights under $50, carried them daily for 30 days, and ran them through a battery of tests (pun intended). Here's what we found.

Our testing methodology: Each flashlight was carried in a pocket for at least 6 hours daily, used for 3+ tasks per day (finding dropped items, walking the dog, power outage simulation), dropped from waist height onto concrete 3 times, and left on max output until thermal step-down. We tested actual output with a lux meter at 1 meter distance.

Buying Guide: How to Choose an EDC Flashlight

Lumens vs. Real-World Use

Don't get caught up in the lumen race. 200-500 lumens is more than enough for most EDC tasks — finding things in dark spaces, walking to your car, reading a map. Anything above 1000 lumens is fun but rarely necessary, and it comes at the cost of battery life and heat management.

Battery Type Matters

AA and AAA batteries are convenient because you can buy them anywhere. 18650 lithium-ion batteries offer much higher capacity and output, but you need to charge them (USB-C charging on the light is ideal). If you want zero maintenance, go with AA/AAA. If you want maximum performance, go 18650 with USB-C.

Waterproofing Ratings Explained

  • IPX4: Splash resistant (rain, no submersion) — adequate for most EDC
  • IPX8: Submersible (usually 2m for 30 min) — best for outdoor/water use
  • IP66: Dust-tight + powerful water jets — great for rugged use

Weight and Pocket Carry

If you're carrying this light every day in your pocket, every ounce matters. The Streamlight MicroStream at 1.2 oz is the lightest you'll forget it's there. The Sofirn SC31 Pro at 3.35 oz will definitely remind you it's in your pocket.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many lumens do I need for an EDC flashlight?

For everyday carry, 200-500 lumens is plenty for most tasks. Higher lumen counts (1000+) are great for outdoor use but drain batteries faster and generate more heat. We recommend prioritizing beam quality and runtime over raw lumen output.

What battery type is best for an EDC flashlight?

AA and AAA batteries are the most convenient since you can buy them anywhere. 18650 rechargeable batteries offer higher output and runtime but require a dedicated charger or USB charging built into the light. For zero-maintenance carry, go AA/AAA. For maximum performance, go 18650 with USB-C.

What is IPX8 waterproofing?

IPX8 means the flashlight can be submerged in water beyond 1 meter depth (typically up to 2 meters for 30 minutes). This is the highest common waterproof rating for EDC flashlights and means you don't have to worry about rain, drops in puddles, or accidental washing.

Are these flashlights legal to carry?

Yes, all flashlights in this review are legal to carry in all 50 US states and most countries. There are no restrictions on flashlight brightness or carrying in public spaces, unlike knives or other tools.

How long do rechargeable flashlight batteries last?

A quality 18650 battery typically lasts 500+ charge cycles before significant capacity loss. That's about 2-3 years of daily use. Built-in batteries (like in the ThruNite TIP2) may degrade faster since they can't be replaced — expect 1-2 years before noticeable capacity loss.
