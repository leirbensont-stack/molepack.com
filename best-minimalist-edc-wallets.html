The fat leather bifold is dying. A minimalist wallet — slim, light, and built for cards and a few bills — is the modern EDC standard. But "minimalist" means very different things depending on the brand, from machined metal plates to pop-up card mechanisms to simple fabric sleeves.

We carried 5 of the most popular minimalist wallets for 60 days, testing card capacity, RFID blocking, durability, and — most importantly — how each actually felt in a front pocket. Here's what earned its space.

Our testing methodology: Each wallet was carried in a front pocket for 60 days. We tested card insertion/removal speed and ease, verified RFID blocking with an NFC reader, dropped each wallet 50 times from waist height, and checked for card damage and material wear throughout.

Buying Guide: How to Choose a Minimalist Wallet

Material: Metal vs. Fabric vs. Leather

Metal (aluminum, titanium) protects cards from bending and snapping, and lasts essentially forever — but it's stiff and can scratch a phone. Fabric is featherlight and soft, but offers no card protection. Leather looks premium and ages well, but needs occasional care and shows wear.

How Many Cards Do You Actually Carry?

Be honest about your card count. Most people carry 6-8 cards: ID, 1-2 debit/credit cards, a transit pass, and a couple of loyalty cards. Buying a 12-card wallet when you only carry 6 just means you'll overstuff it later.

RFID Blocking: Nice-to-Have, Not Essential

RFID skimming is rare and banks cover fraud, but RFID blocking is a cheap insurance policy. If you travel or carry contactless cards, it's worth having. Nearly all metal wallets include it; fabric wallets may not.

Cash: Do You Still Use It?

If you still carry cash, look for a wallet with a money clip, strap, or fold. Metal plate wallets handle bills awkwardly, while fabric sleeves usually have a center fold. Pick based on your actual cash habits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need RFID blocking in a wallet?

For most people, RFID blocking is a nice-to-have rather than a necessity. Contactless card skimming is rare and banks cover fraudulent charges. However, if you carry RFID-enabled cards or passports and travel frequently, an RFID-blocking wallet adds a layer of peace of mind.

How many cards can a minimalist wallet hold?

Most minimalist wallets hold 4-12 cards. A good rule of thumb is to pick a wallet that holds 6-8 cards, since that covers the essentials (ID, 1-2 debit/credit cards, a transit pass, and a couple of loyalty cards) without tempting you to overstuff.

Are metal wallets safe for cards?

Yes, if used properly. Metal wallets protect cards from bending and snapping, but you should avoid overstuffing them, which can scratch cards or damage the magnetic stripes. They can also scratch your phone or other items if carried in the same pocket.

Will a minimalist wallet stretch out my cards or cash?

No. In fact, minimalist wallets generally keep cards flatter than overstuffed bifolds. Just avoid forcing more cards than the wallet is rated for, and keep bills folded neatly to prevent bulk and card warping.
