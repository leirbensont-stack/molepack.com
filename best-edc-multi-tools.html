A multi-tool is the single most useful object you can put in your pocket. It replaces a knife, pliers, screwdrivers, scissors, and a bottle opener — and it's there the moment something breaks, loosens, or needs opening. But not all multi-tools are created equal, and the wrong one will rattle in a drawer while you reach for a dedicated tool.

We tested 5 of the most popular EDC multi-tools for 30 days, putting each through 15 standardized tasks: cutting wire, driving screws, gripping bolts, opening cans and bottles, cutting cardboard and paracord, and a 3-foot drop test. Here's which ones earned a permanent spot on our belts.

Our testing methodology: Each multi-tool was carried daily for 30 days and used for a minimum of 3 tasks per day. We tested every implement (pliers, knife, scissors, screwdrivers, saw, file) on representative materials, measured plier grip on hex bolts, and dropped each tool from waist height onto concrete 3 times to test lock-up and hinge durability.

Buying Guide: How to Choose a Multi-Tool

Pliers vs. Knife-Centric

The biggest decision is what you'll actually use it for. If you fix things, grip bolts, cut wire, or work mechanically, get a pliers-based tool (Leatherman, Gerber, SOG). If you mostly open packages, cut string, and do light tasks, a knife-centric Swiss Army Knife is lighter and more refined.

Outside-Accessible Tools

The best multi-tools let you open the knife and scissors without unfolding the pliers. This one feature separates a tool you'll use constantly from one that lives in a drawer.

Locking Tools

Locking blades and tools are safer under load and generally required if the blade is over a certain length. Non-locking (slipjoint) tools are lighter and legal in more places, but you must be careful with them.

Weight vs. Capability

There's a direct trade-off: the Wave+ gives you full-size pliers at 8.5 oz, while the Huntsman gives you 90% of daily utility at 3.4 oz. Decide how much weight you're willing to carry every single day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a multi-tool worth carrying every day?

Yes, for most people. A multi-tool replaces a pocket knife, pliers, screwdrivers, scissors, and a bottle opener in one compact package. If you regularly fix things, open packages, or work with your hands, it's the highest-utility item you can carry.

Leatherman vs Swiss Army Knife: which is better?

It depends on the tool you need most. Leatherman-style tools center on full-size pliers, making them better for gripping, wire-cutting, and mechanical work. Swiss Army Knives center on a knife and scissors, making them lighter and better for everyday small tasks and food prep.

Are multi-tools legal to carry?

In most places, yes, but the knife blade inside a multi-tool is still subject to knife laws. Check blade-length and locking-blade restrictions in your area. Most multi-tools are under the common 3-inch limit, but some areas restrict locking blades.

How long do multi-tools last?

A quality multi-tool from Leatherman, Gerber, SOG, or Victorinox will last decades with basic care. Leatherman and Victorinox back this up with 25-year and lifetime warranties respectively. Budget tools from no-name brands often develop hinge play and dull steel within a year or two.

Final Thoughts

After 30 days and hundreds of tasks, the Leatherman Wave+ remains the multi-tool to beat. It's the most capable, best-built, and most confidently warrantied tool you can carry — and it justifies its $120 price.

For most people, though, the Gerber Suspension NXT at $40 is the smarter buy. It does nearly everything the Wave+ does, with outside-accessible tools, for a third of the price. And if you want the lightest, friendliest option, the Victorinox Huntsman is a century of refinement in your pocket.

The best multi-tool is the one you'll actually carry. Pick the weight and tool set that match your real life, and it'll pay for itself the first time it saves a trip to the toolbox.
