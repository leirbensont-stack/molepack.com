The Benchmade Bugout is the knife that defined the "ultra-light EDC" category — and then Benchmade did something smart: they made a smaller version. The Mini Bugout keeps everything people love about the original and shrinks it down to 1.5 ounces.

But smaller isn't automatically better. We carried both for 60 days, cut hundreds of boxes and cords, and compared them head-to-head. Here's which one earns a permanent spot in your pocket.

Our testing methodology: Both knives were carried daily for 60 days and used for 10+ cutting tasks per week (cardboard, zip ties, paracord, food prep). We measured edge retention with a standardized cardboard-cutting sequence, tested the AXIS lock for blade play after a 3-foot drop, and evaluated pocket comfort and one-hand deployment across both sizes.

Quick Verdict

Buy the full-size Bugout if:

You have medium-to-large hands, want a blade long enough for food prep and heavy cardboard, and don't mind spending a bit more.

Buy the Mini Bugout if:

You have smaller hands, want the absolute lightest carry possible, or live somewhere with a sub-3-inch blade restriction.

Head-to-Head: 5 Key Differences

1. Blade Length: 3.24" vs 2.82"

This is the single biggest difference. The full-size Bugout's 3.24-inch blade gives you noticeably more cutting edge for slicing food, breaking down boxes, and general work. The Mini's 2.82-inch blade handles everyday tasks fine, but it's short for things like slicing an apple or a bagel in one stroke. If food prep matters, go full-size.

2. Weight: 1.85 oz vs 1.5 oz

Both are absurdly light, but the Mini is on another level. At 1.5 oz, it genuinely disappears. If you wear lightweight shorts or athletic pants, the Mini won't sag a pocket at all. The Bugout is still featherweight by any normal standard.

3. Ergonomics and Hand Size

The Bugout's 7.46-inch overall length gives you a full four-finger grip for most hands. The Mini at 6.49 inches is a comfortable three-and-a-half-finger grip for medium hands, and a full grip for small hands. If you wear a large or XL glove, the full-size is the better fit.

4. Legal Considerations

Many cities (Boston, Chicago, New York) restrict blades over 3 inches. The Mini's 2.82-inch blade sneaks under that line, making it legal to carry in more places. If you travel between jurisdictions or work in a city with strict knife laws, the Mini is the safer choice.

5. Price: $180 vs $150

There's a $30 gap, but both are premium knives. If budget is the deciding factor, the Mini saves you $30 without sacrificing steel or build quality.

Who Should Buy Which

Buy the Bugout 535 if…

  • You have medium to large hands
  • You want a blade long enough for food prep
  • You do heavier cutting tasks regularly
  • Blade-length laws aren't a concern

Buy the Mini Bugout 533 if…

  • You have smaller hands
  • You want the lightest carry possible
  • You live or travel where blades under 3" are required
  • You want a less intimidating office carry

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Benchmade Bugout worth the money?

Yes, for most EDC users. The Bugout delivers premium S30V steel and a flawless AXIS lock at under 2 ounces, making it one of the lightest full-size EDC knives available. It's expensive, but it's a buy-it-once knife.

What is the difference between the Bugout and Mini Bugout?

The main differences are size and weight. The Bugout has a 3.24-inch blade at 1.85 oz, while the Mini Bugout has a 2.82-inch blade at 1.5 oz. Both use S30V steel and the AXIS lock. The Mini is better for small hands and light carry; the full-size is better for larger hands and heavier cutting.

Is the Mini Bugout too small?

For most daily tasks, no. The Mini Bugout's 2.82-inch blade handles boxes, envelopes, cord, and food prep easily. It only feels small if you have large hands or do heavy cutting work, in which case the full-size Bugout is the better fit.

Is the Grivory handle durable?

Yes. Grivory is a glass-reinforced nylon that's tough and rigid for its weight. It won't feel like G10 or titanium, and it can flex slightly under hard use, but it's far more durable than its "plasticky" feel suggests and keeps the knife feather-light.

Final Verdict

Both knives are outstanding, and you can't go wrong. But if we had to pick one for the broadest range of people, the full-size Bugout 535 wins on versatility — its 3.24-inch blade handles everything from boxes to food prep, and its weight is already negligible.

That said, if you have smaller hands, want the absolute lightest carry, or need to stay under a 3-inch blade limit, the Mini Bugout 533 is the perfect choice — same steel, same lock, $30 cheaper, and it vanishes in your pocket.

The Bugout family earns its reputation. Pick your size, and you'll be carrying one of the best EDC knives ever made.
